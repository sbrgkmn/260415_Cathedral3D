01 · Branching tree

Fork angle is measured between the two children. Each generation scales that angle by the fork angle ratio and scales its branch length by the child ratio. Lengths use model units.

Leaf-like marginal growth

Symmetric lobes and notches break perimeter edges before vertical E/C. Roof and walls share the new outline; the underside stays flat. Smaller edges stop early.

Nave widths and vertical development

Each terminal end projects into a mirrored two-face nose. Tip faces use surface area for E/C stopping so steep faces can develop despite their small footprint.

Side width is a fraction of the spine width, compounded at each branching order. Side clear length scales the projections beyond their junctions. These controls apply with thickness reservation enabled.

Continuous offset sides replace the pinched collars. Thickening reserves additional length at junctions and can spread the tree to keep recesses open. Length controls supply the clear branch spans; use Fit after width edits. Uncheck to compare the previous fixed-plan construction.

Spine and projection widths are independent. With thickness reservation enabled, width is applied directly; junction spacing expands instead of silently reducing the width. Each cycle expands a shoulder and contracts a notch along slopes toward local tips. Shared edges split together; minimum plan area stops small triangles early. Vertical gables remain unchanged.

Recursive growth rules

Fullness widens the branch collars and junctions. Positive angle increments open later forks: angle = initial angle � ratio^step + increment � step. Local clearance preserves gaps.

Long edges accumulate paired side branches at regular intervals. Each pass repeats on those branches. Peaks descend from their attachment height; forks stay open.

02 · Peak hierarchy

Height ratio acts on rise above the eaves. 100% keeps peaks level; larger values raise the outer peaks.

Every peak belongs to the tree. Each branch keeps its own mirrored roof wedges and side surfaces. Lower the child height ratio to make a steeper mountain profile.

Each branch thickens within its own available space. Junctions taper to keep the forks and gaps identifiable.

This study establishes the form before introducing E/C transformations.
